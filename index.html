Design foundation
Assumptions

Every learning design rests on assumptions. Naming them makes the design honest — and easier to revise when reality differs.

ASSUMPTION 01

Learner identity

The scenario character is named "Alex" — a short form that works across English, Latvian, Danish and German. In reality, this work is performed by both women and men. The character does not define who the course is for.

⚠ To be validated
ASSUMPTION 02

Learning context

It is unknown whether employees study independently, alongside a mentor, or with a supervisor. This course is designed to work in any of these scenarios — as pre-task preparation or as a complement to on-the-job guidance.

⚠ To be validated
ASSUMPTION 03

Ideal course design

This prototype reflects the best possible version of this course, without limitation by any specific platform's capabilities. Platform constraints (e.g. Hihaho) will be applied in a later stage.

What learners will achieve
Learning objectives

By the end of this course, learners will be able to:

🦺
Work safely at every stepIdentify required PPE for lifting and packing, follow all preparation steps in order, and apply non-negotiable safety rules — including the centre of gravity sticker before removing the crane.
🏗️
Lift and pack a finished conveyor correctlyExecute the full lifting procedure and full packing procedure without skipping or reversing any step.
🔗
Understand how the two instructions connectExplain the handoff point where lifting ends and packing begins, and recognise that a worker assigned to pack must first confirm lifting was completed correctly.

✓ Expected learning outcome

After all three modules, a learner can independently and safely prepare a finished Caljan conveyor for dispatch — from production stand to fully packed and labelled.

Slide 3 of 5 · Decision 2 — Critical handoff

The moment the two instructions connect

Lifting done. Conveyor is on the trolley. Alex is about to remove the crane and start packing — when he stops.

"Wait. The gravity sticker."

He checks. Left side has the sticker. Right side is bare. The packing film is in his hand. He is behind schedule.
❓
Choose the correct action.
A
Remove crane and start packing — sticker can go on after.
B
Ask a colleague to apply the sticker while he starts packing.
C
Sticker on both sides → remove crane → begin packing.
Slide 4 of 5 · Decision 3 — Spot the mistake

Something is wrong — can you see it?

📷Film melting — regular gloves visible
→ <img src="combined-spot-mistake.jpg">
Alex is melting film. A colleague stops:
"Alex — where are your gloves?"

He stopped to write the serial number, put the thermal gloves down, and forgot to put them back on. He is now melting film with regular work gloves.
❓
Choose the correct action.
A
Continue carefully — almost done with this section.
B
Work faster to finish before heat is a problem.
C
Stop, put machine down, put on thermal gloves, then continue.

What you have learned

  • The correct PPE for both lifting and packing operations
  • The full lifting procedure — retract, disconnect, deattach, sling, crane, trolley, sticker
  • The full packing procedure — protective materials, two film layers, identification, stickers, paint
  • The gravity sticker rule: both sides, before the crane is removed — no exceptions
  • Thermal gloves are mandatory for all film melting — no exceptions
  • Both instructions form one connected process: lifting ends where packing begins
